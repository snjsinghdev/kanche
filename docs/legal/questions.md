# Questions for counsel: Kanche and India's online gaming law

**Status:** draft for the owner to review and send (Gate 3, `docs/gates.md`). The written answer is needed before Phase 6 starts. This is not legal advice: it sets out the facts and asks the questions.

**Drafted:** 2026-10-17 by Claude Code.

**The law as reported, which counsel should confirm:**

- the Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act, 2025;
- the Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Rules, 2026, reportedly notified on 22 April 2026 and in force from 1 May 2026, together with the Act's commencement and the constitution of the Online Gaming Authority of India;
- the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and its Rules, for players under 18.

## The facts about Kanche

Kanche is a mobile and web game of street marbles ("kanche"), for Android, iOS and the browser, played against computer-controlled neighbourhood kids and against friends.

1. **Free to play, with one optional purchase.** A one-time unlock (about ₹99) buys more content, such as extra neighbourhoods and cosmetic options. It never buys marbles, stakes, odds, better results or any advantage in a match. Every mode, for-keeps play included, can be played without it. There are no subscriptions, no loot boxes and no paid randomness of any kind.
2. **Marbles are virtual, and earned only by playing.** Each marble is a unique virtual item with a recorded history. Marbles come only from free play: winning matches and in-game events. They cannot be bought, sold, traded for money, gifted, or exchanged for money, credits or anything outside the game. The game's server records ownership in an append-only ledger, and the player's device never writes it.
3. **"For keeps" play.** As in the street game, players may agree before a match to play for keeps: each puts marbles into the match, and the winner takes them. Only marbles earned in free play can be put in, and what changes hands is only those virtual marbles, never money. A player can always choose a friendly match, where nothing changes hands.
4. **The furnace.** Players craft new glass marbles in an in-game furnace, from materials earned in play. Furnace marbles are keepsakes: they cannot be staked, sold, traded or gifted. Furnace materials are never sold.
5. **No prize tournaments.** We plan no tournaments with money or real-world prizes, and no cash or prize rewards of any kind.
6. **Children.** A child mode for players under 18 limits the data collected, the stakes available and the notifications sent. Any playtests with under-18s are run only with a parent's consent.
7. **Communication** between players is preset emotes only: no free text and no voice.
8. **Distribution** is through Google Play, the Apple App Store and the web, where a link shared on WhatsApp opens the game in a browser.

## The questions

1. **Is Kanche an "online money game"?**
   - Is Kanche, or its for-keeps mode, an "online money game" within the Act's prohibition?
   - In particular, are virtual marbles that can only be earned in free play, have no monetary value, and can never be bought, sold, traded, gifted or cashed out, "stakes", "winnings" or "enrichment in kind"?
2. **Does the unlock change the answer?** Given the facts in point 1, does the one-time paid unlock affect that classification, since it buys content only and is not needed for for-keeps play?
3. **Is Kanche an "online social game"?**
   - Is Kanche an "online social game" under the Act and the 2026 Rules?
   - Must it register? We understand registration applies to social games the Centre notifies on grounds such as risk to users (especially children), scale, volume of financial transactions and the platform's country of origin.
   - Is voluntary registration available, and would you advise it?
4. **Should we seek a determination?** Should Kanche ask the Online Gaming Authority of India to confirm that it is not an online money game, before launch or after? What would that process involve, and how long would it take?
5. **What do the Act and Rules require if Kanche is lawful?** If Kanche is a lawful social game, what obligations apply? We have in mind registration certificates, age verification, grievance redressal, record-keeping and disclosures, and anything else the Act or Rules require.
6. **Players under 18:**
   - What do the Act, the 2026 Rules and the DPDP Act require for players under 18? We have in mind verifiable parental consent, and limits on tracking, profiling and targeted advertising.
   - Is our child mode enough, and which methods of age assurance would you accept?
7. **Trading outside the game:** if some players sell accounts or marbles outside the game for money, against our terms, does that change Kanche's classification or create liability for us? What terms and enforcement would you expect us to have in place?
8. **Free events:** may Kanche run free events, or leaderboards with in-game rewards only (marbles, cosmetics), without becoming a money game or needing registration?
9. **Advertising:** are there limits on advertising or promoting a game that has a "for keeps" mode? What wording should we avoid in store listings and promotions?
10. **What would change the answer?** Is anything else in the facts above likely to change your answer, so we can avoid it in the design?

## What we need back

- **A written opinion** on questions 1–3: the classification, and whether to register.
- **An answer on registration,** and on a determination (question 4).
- **A list of the obligations** in questions 5–9 that we must build into the game before launch.
